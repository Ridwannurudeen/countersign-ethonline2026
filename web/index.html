That was Countersign: a paid authorization review against a policy the treasury owner signed. Approval adds the guard's signature. Refusal leaves it absent. Hedera enforces the treasury's key tree.

The agent can publish the transfer it wants, but its key alone satisfies neither branch. The same treasury can also pay an ordinary x402 seller: the agent and guard sign the purchase, through an unmodified facilitator. The owner does not sign each purchase.

Why your key needs another signature

The rule is not in the agent's code, where a compromised agent could skip it. It is in the treasury account's key list, which Hedera evaluates at consensus for every transaction that spends from the account. 

treasury 0.0.10502365
1-of[
  ownerKey,
  2-of[ agentKey, guardKey ]
]
Who signs Branch satisfied What Hedera does
Owner alone First member of the 1-of Executes. The owner can always recover funds without the guard.
Agent alone Neither: the 2-of is half complete A scheduled transfer waits unexecuted. A direct transfer is rejected with INVALID_SIGNATURE.
Guard alone Neither Nothing. The guard cannot move the funds it protects.
Agent and guard Second member of the 1-of Executes.

The guard process holds the guard key; it cannot spend the treasury on its own. For the sandbox encounter, a separate operator service holds the agent and payer keys and pre-signed owner authorizations. The owner key stays offline.

What a refusal leaves behind.

The common design for agent spending controls makes the decision inside a vendor's own boundary and records a denial in the vendor's own logs. To confirm that a transfer was blocked, you ask the vendor.

Countersign's refusal is the absence of a signature on a public ledger. The schedule exists, its executed_timestamp is null, and the guard's key prefix is missing from signatures[]. Anyone can confirm that with one unauthenticated request to the Hedera mirror node, without running our code or taking our word for it.

Policy engines inside a vendor boundary
AWS Bedrock AgentCore Payments, Fireblocks, Coinbase, Turnkey, Google AP2		 Countersign
Where the check runs Inside the vendor's service. At Hedera consensus, by the treasury's own key tree. The guard adds one signature; it never executes anything itself.
Where a denial is recorded In the vendor's private logs. On the public ledger: an unexecuted schedule with no guard signature, plus a verdict record on a Hedera Consensus Service topic.
Who can verify a refusal The vendor, and whoever it grants access. Anyone with HTTP. No account with us, no API key, no client library.

What happened during the wait

The schedule path, as it ran on testnet. Prices and endpoints are read from the live service and dated; they are configuration, not constants.

  1. The agent publishes what it wants to do

    It submits the transfer as a Hedera Scheduled Transaction carrying only its own signature. The schedule is public and unexecuted, and its memo is the SHA-256 digest of the owner-signed mandate it claims to satisfy.

  2. The caller asks for a review and gets a price

    POST /review answers 402 Payment Required. On 2026-09-12 the agent card at /.well-known/agent.json quoted 1,000,000 tinybars on hedera:testnet, exact scheme, paid up front. There is no metering after the fact.

  3. The fee settles before any work is done

    Payment goes through the Blocky402 x402 facilitator from an account that holds none of the treasury's keys. The guard refuses at startup if its fee destination is the treasury, the owner, the agent or itself.

  4. The guard reads consensus, not the caller

    It resolves the ScheduleID from a Hedera consensus node and never trusts a summary sent with the request. It reads the node's protobuf and services versions before and after, and refuses on any mismatch with its reviewed allowlist, so a schema change fails closed instead of approving a field it cannot see.

    Every decoded field is then checked against the mandate: recipient allowlist, amount cap, HBAR-only asset, exact fee, memo binding, expiry, creator, payer, the agent's signature present and the guard's absent.

  5. Approve or refuse. The fee is kept either way.

    Approved

    Every check passed. The guard submits ScheduleSign, the 2-of branch completes, and Hedera executes the transfer.

    Refused

    One check failed. The guard signs nothing. The schedule sits unexecuted until it expires, and the treasury balance does not move.

  6. The verdict is written where you can read it

    Approved or refused, a compact record naming the ScheduleID, mandate digest, x402 settlement and both participants' HCS-14 identifiers is published to an immutable Consensus Service topic, and the HTTP response links to it.

Where this authorization ends

It is not on mainnet. Every account, schedule, payment and verdict above is on Hedera testnet. It has no users: all 10 schedule-path reviews and both buyer-path runs were made by the operator. The demonstration seller is ours, running stock x402 libraries; no external merchant and no customer exist. The treasuries are enrolled by the operator, and the guard is listed in no directory.

The full list, with what would have to change for each, is on the limitations page.
